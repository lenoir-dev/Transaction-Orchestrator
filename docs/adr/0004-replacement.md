# ADR 0004: Bounded replacement

Status: Accepted design.

An ordinary replacement is another signed attempt of the same intent and nonce. Chain ID, signer, destination, value, calldata, gas limit, access list, and request/policy authorization remain identical. Only `maxPriorityFeePerGas` and `maxFeePerGas` change. No implicit cancel-to-self or changed business action is allowed. Each attempt's bytes and hash are immutable.

Implemented policy takes a caller-supplied minimum interval and positive `bumpBps` (1–100000), and uses the per-intent replacement count. Both prior tip cap and fee cap become `max(previous + 1, ceil(previous × (10000 + bumpBps) / 10000))` with exact integer arithmetic. This rounds up and strictly increases even a zero prior value. The new tip must not exceed the fee cap; both are checked against per-intent ceilings and the stored gas-budget cap. A ceiling or count limit enters `BLOCKED` with a stable reason and stops escalation. No value is silently clamped. The test policy uses 1500 bps and 120 seconds; the library sets no deployment default. This is a candidate for local testing, not a universal pool-admission guarantee. Live base-fee pricing and head freshness are not implemented.

An exclusive fenced replacement claim is committed before signing. Query every known hash/receipt and latest account nonce before claiming, then again before broadcast; a race can still mine the old attempt, so keep watching **all** signed hashes. A replacement signed after the old transaction mined may be persisted but must not be sent when canonical evidence consumes the nonce. If the original mines while a replacement is in flight, a matching observed receipt selects the provisional winner; the other remains an immutable losing attempt. One endpoint's underpriced rejection does not mean the original was dropped; record rejection, continue observation, and reconsider only after the configured interval and budget. Conflicting RPC observations block fee escalation until rechecked. Receipt canonicality and confirmation progression are handled by ADR 0006; inclusion status and EVM execution outcome remain separate. A reverted execution is not repaired by fee bump.

Replacement is safe only when the original call remains authorized, the nonce is owned by this intent, no canonical receipt/foreign consumption is established, the bounded policy allows it, and observation before sending is sufficiently fresh. These checks cannot rule out an in-flight original; identical call/value semantics and same nonce make only one attempt executable on one canonical chain. Bounded receipt reorg recovery and confirmation policy are implemented by the receipt service. Separate explicit cancellation requires its own authorization and model and is outside v1.

Go-ethereum exposes `--txpool.pricebump` as a configurable transaction-pool setting (10% in the referenced CLI), and its legacy pool replacement check compares both fee cap and tip against the pool threshold: [CLI documentation](https://geth.ethereum.org/docs/fundamentals/command-line-options) and [source](https://github.com/ethereum/go-ethereum/blob/master/core/txpool/legacypool/list.go). Endpoint/client settings may differ; an underpriced response records a rejection and never causes an unbounded immediate retry.

# ADR 0002: Durable nonce ownership

Status: Accepted design.

One orchestrator authority exclusively owns each configured `(chain_id, signer_address)` account. All replicas share PostgreSQL. Keys must not be used for independent submissions; if that contract cannot be enforced, pause allocation and require reconciliation. The signer exposes only an allowlisted account and cannot allocate a nonce. PostgreSQL is the correctness authority for local allocations, not an in-memory lock or one RPC's pending count.

The account row is locked with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`. Under the same transaction, choose the next unallocated nonce at or above a verified chain `latest` count and the durable high-water cursor, insert one reservation, update the cursor, and bind the intent. Unique `(chain_id, signer_address, nonce)` and unique `intent_id` constraints make concurrent replicas safe. RPC `pending` count is advisory and can be stale or include external transactions; if it exceeds the local cursor, pause and investigate the intervening nonces before advancing. Reads happen before the short DB transaction and are checked for freshness against a configured head/chain anchor. A post-read change may still occur; exclusive key ownership is therefore a deployment invariant.

On restart, reconstruct ownership from reservations and account rows, resume every incomplete intent, query all attempt hashes/receipts, then inspect chain counts. `latest` greater than a reserved nonce means it was consumed on the canonical chain, but does not identify which transaction; find a known receipt or block transaction evidence. If none matches, mark `EXTERNAL_NONCE_CONFLICT` and block, never label our intent successful. A conflicting external submission or signer misuse triggers an incident and stops new reservations. A gap caused by a pre-sign crash remains reserved for its intent and is resumed; later nonces should not be broadcast past a blocked gap without policy review.

Reservations are never deleted or automatically reused, even after failed or orphaned attempts. A nonce reserved but never signed may be explicitly reclaimed only through a separately audited operator workflow that proves no signed bytes were persisted or sent, no external tx used the nonce, and no later reservation depends on it. That workflow is outside v1; v1 leaves the gap blocked. A mined reorg can reopen a formerly consumed nonce, so confirmation never releases its reservation. The only permitted same-nonce competing signed transactions are attempts of the same intent.
